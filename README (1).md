# Member Lifetime Value for a Travel Subscription Programme

How much is a new member worth, which acquisition channels bring the most valuable members, and how confident can we be?

This project estimates **customer lifetime value (LTV)** for an annual travel membership (a Prime-style programme: members pay a yearly fee, book discounted travel while active, and decide at each anniversary whether to renew). It models renewals with a **shifted Beta-Geometric (sBG)** model, compares LTV with acquisition cost by channel, back-tests the forecasts, and handles a new channel with very little history.

> **All data is synthetic.** The dataset is generated by `src/simulate.py` with invented parameters. It is shaped like a travel subscription business, but none of the numbers describe a real company.

## Business questions

1. What is a new member worth over five years, by acquisition channel?
2. Which channels pay back their acquisition cost, and how quickly?
3. How much does the choice of retention model change the answer?
4. How should we value a **new channel** (here, members arriving through conversational AI assistants) that has almost no renewal history?
5. Where is future value concentrated among current members?

## Why the sBG model

Membership is **contractual and discrete**: churn is observed (the member doesn't renew) and can only happen at the yearly renewal date. That is exactly the setting the sBG model was designed for (Fader & Hardie, 2007).

- Each member has their own probability `p` of cancelling at each renewal.
- `p` varies across members following a Beta(a, b) distribution.
- Survival after `t` renewals: `S(t) = B(a, b + t) / B(a, b)`.

The key insight: **the members most likely to cancel leave first**, so the remaining base becomes more loyal every year and the aggregate renewal rate rises with tenure. The common shortcut of applying the first-year churn rate to every year misses this and understates long-term value.

## Data

`data/members.csv`: one row per member, 64,525 members, cohorts April 2021 to March 2025, observed to 31 March 2026.

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `member_id` | Member identifier |
| `channel` | Acquisition channel: Organic / SEO, Paid search, Metasearch, App, Conversational AI |
| `join_date`, `cohort_fy` | Sign-up date and fiscal-year cohort (April to March) |
| `renewal_opportunities` | Anniversaries passed by the observation date |
| `renewals` | Renewals completed |
| `churned` | Whether the member has cancelled (False = still active, right-censored) |
| `margin_per_active_year` | Contribution per active year: fee plus booking margin minus servicing cost (EUR) |

Conversational AI is a new channel that starts in October 2024, so its members have reached at most one renewal.

## Method

```
members.csv
   │
   ├─► Observed retention by channel (handles right-censoring)
   │
   ├─► sBG fit per channel (maximum likelihood) ─► S(t)
   │        │
   │        └─► 5-year LTV = Σ margin × S(t) / 1.10^t,  t = 0..4
   │                 │
   │                 └─► LTV:CAC and payback by channel
   │
   ├─► Back-test: fit on data as of 31 Mar 2024, forecast FY22 cohort
   │   retention at renewals 3 and 4, compare sBG vs. constant churn
   │
   ├─► New channel: bootstrap the year-1 rate + borrow later-year
   │   retention shapes from mature channels (scenario range)
   │
   └─► Active members: expected next-3-year value, updated by tenure
```

**LTV definition**
- **Margin, not revenue**, so it can be compared with acquisition cost
- **Per acquired member**, including those who cancel after year one
- **5-year horizon**, discounted at 10% per year; year-0 margin arrives at sign-up
- **Payback** = CAC ÷ monthly margin, assuming margin accrues evenly through the year

**Fitting the sBG model** (`src/sbg.py`). Each member contributes either the probability of cancelling at renewal `k` or, if still active, the probability of surviving `T` renewals (right-censoring):

```python
def log_likelihood(params, renewals, churned):
    a, b = np.exp(params)
    base = betaln(a, b)
    ll_churn = betaln(a + 1, b + renewals) - base   # cancelled at renewal k = renewals + 1
    ll_alive = betaln(a, b + renewals) - base       # still active after `renewals` renewals
    return np.where(churned, ll_churn, ll_alive).sum()
```

**Back-testing without leakage.** `as_of()` rebuilds the dataset as it looked on 31 March 2024, hiding every later renewal or cancellation. Models are fitted on that view and compared with what the FY22 cohort actually did in years 3 and 4.

**Updating value with tenure.** A member who has renewed `n` times has revealed they are lower-risk, so their churn probability follows Beta(a, b + n). Expected future value is computed from that conditional curve.

## Results

### LTV by channel (5-year horizon, 10% discount rate)

| Channel | Members | 1st renewal | Active after 4 renewals | Margin / year | 5-yr LTV | CAC | LTV:CAC | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic / SEO | 15,356 | 65% | 30% | €62 | €152 | €22 | 6.9 | 4 mo |
| Conversational AI | 554 | 66% | n/a | €63 | €150\* | €30 | 5.0 | 6 mo |
| App | 10,450 | 72% | 40% | €74 | €205 | €41 | 5.0 | 7 mo |
| Paid search | 20,082 | 52% | 17% | €58 | €118 | €46 | 2.6 | 10 mo |
| Metasearch | 18,083 | 40% | 9% | €49 | €83 | €36 | 2.3 | 9 mo |

\* Midpoint of a scenario range (see below).

![LTV vs CAC](figures/02_ltv_vs_cac.png)

- **App members are the most valuable** (€205): they renew best and spend the most.
- **Organic / SEO has the best return** (LTV:CAC 6.9) because it is cheap to acquire.
- **Paid search and metasearch** are profitable but much thinner (2.3 to 2.6), and metasearch loses 60% of members at the first renewal.

### Back-test

Forecasts made with data available at 31 March 2024, compared with what the FY22 cohort actually did:

| Channel | Renewal | Actual | sBG | Constant churn | sBG error | Constant error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic / SEO | 3 | 35.2% | 35.9% | 26.8% | +0.7 pp | -8.4 pp |
| Organic / SEO | 4 | 28.6% | 29.0% | 17.3% | +0.3 pp | -11.4 pp |
| Paid search | 3 | 23.1% | 24.2% | 13.8% | +1.1 pp | -9.3 pp |
| Paid search | 4 | 17.4% | 18.7% | 7.1% | +1.4 pp | -10.2 pp |
| Metasearch | 3 | 13.4% | 13.4% | 6.5% | -0.1 pp | -6.9 pp |
| Metasearch | 4 | 8.8% | 9.1% | 2.6% | +0.3 pp | -6.1 pp |
| App | 3 | 46.7% | 47.3% | 37.9% | +0.6 pp | -8.8 pp |
| App | 4 | 40.3% | 40.5% | 27.4% | +0.3 pp | -12.9 pp |

![Back-test](figures/03_backtest.png)

Mean absolute error: **sBG 0.6 pp vs. constant churn 9.3 pp**. Applying first-year churn to every year understates 5-year LTV by about **8%** on average, and the gap grows with longer horizons.

> The sBG fit is close partly because the synthetic data was generated from the same family of model. On real data, expect larger errors (seasonality, price changes, product launches), but the direction of the constant-churn bias holds: it always understates retention when members differ in loyalty.

### Valuing a new channel with little history

Conversational AI members (554) have reached one renewal at most. Their first-renewal rate is **66%** (bootstrapped 90% interval: 62% to 69%), but the data says nothing yet about years two to four, so an sBG fit on this channel alone is not reliable.

Instead, the year-1 rate is combined with the later-year retention **shape** of each mature channel:

| Assumed later-year shape | 5-year LTV |
|---|---|
| Like Metasearch (least loyal) | €136 |
| Like Paid search | €146 |
| Like Organic / SEO | €157 |
| Like App (most loyal) | €164 |

Even the pessimistic case gives **LTV:CAC of 4.5**, so the channel is attractive under every scenario. The decision doesn't need a precise number, only confidence that it clears the bar.

### Value concentration among active members

- The top 10% of active members hold **27%** of expected margin over the next three years.
- Expected 3-year value rises with tenure: **€83** after one renewal, **€96** after two, **€105** after three, **€114** after four. Long-tenured members are both more valuable and more likely to stay.

## Recommendations

1. **Shift acquisition budget toward App and Organic / SEO**, but test increments first: average CAC is not marginal CAC, and the next euro in a channel usually buys less than the average.
2. **Grow the conversational AI channel.** Every reasonable scenario clears LTV:CAC of 4.5. Re-estimate after each renewal wave, and track these cohorts separately.
3. **Fix the first renewal in metasearch.** 60% of metasearch members leave at year one. Test onboarding actions that drive an early booking, measured with an A/B test on renewal, not clicks.
4. **Stop using a single churn rate in LTV.** It understated retention by around 9 percentage points by year four in the back-test, which undervalues the channels with the most loyal members.
5. **Use value-based bidding.** Send channel- and member-level predicted value to ad platforms rather than counting every sign-up equally.
6. **Prioritise retention spend by incremental value, not risk alone.** Use uplift experiments to find members whose renewal an offer actually changes.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic data**: generated from an sBG-like process, so the model fits better than it would on real data.
- **Margin is constant per member over time**: in practice, booking value changes with tenure, seasonality and price.
- **No covariates**: the sBG model doesn't use member behaviour (bookings in the first months, app usage, payment plan). A next step is a gradient-boosting renewal model using these features, with sBG outputs as a baseline.
- **Payment plans**: monthly or quarterly instalments for an annual fee would add mid-year churn points and need a different model structure.
- **Prediction is not causation**: LTV shows where value is; experiments show what changes it.

## How to run

```bash
git clone https://github.com/<your-username>/prime-ltv.git
cd prime-ltv
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate          # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/run_analysis.py
```

This regenerates `data/members.csv`, the tables in `results/` and the charts in `figures/` (about 10 seconds). Change channel assumptions in `src/simulate.py` to explore scenarios.

```

```
